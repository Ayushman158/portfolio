Koi Wishing Pond: a pond that answers your hand

What it is
A koi pond projected onto the floor. Move your hand and the water ripples; make a wish and the koi keep it.
What I did
TouchDesigner build, hand tracking, poster, signs and clues, the film
Team
Ayushman Bharadwaj and Eifa
Response
People stayed, played and came back for another go. Many told us it was their favourite installation.
Play the pond in your browser

Scroll to walk through

Why
I wanted to make a physical experience. Something that makes people stop for a moment, interact with something, and feel happy.

01How you use it

No buttons and nothing to read first. A small sign at the pond shows the only two gestures you need.

Try it in your browser, with your mouse or, if you have a webcam, your hand.

01

Move

Move your hand over the water. Ripples follow it and the koi drift towards you.

02

Hold

Close your hand around a wish and hold it for three seconds. A coin starts to glow.

03

Let go

Open your hand. The coin drops, the koi gather around it, and the pond answers: your wish has been heard.

02How people responded

Wow!

People came in and watched the fish. Some sat and just looked. Others started playing with the koi, and you could hear them go “WOW!” Some came back to do it again.

The faculty and senior management liked it, and a lot of people told us it was their favourite installation.

03Getting people to the room

I designed the poster, the directional signs and a trail of clues around college.

The poster caught people's attention, the clues made them curious, and the arrows brought them to the room.

04The space

We built a space around the projection: the pond, river stones, artificial grass, a bench, candle lanterns and music.

The lab is already dark, so the pond became the only real light in the room.

05How it’s built

Sense

Phone on the floor

An iPhone lies on the floor, its camera looking up at people's hands.

Track

MediaPipe

Finds 21 points on each hand and tells an open hand from a fist.

Decide

TouchDesigner

Turns the hand into ripples, the wish gesture, coins and koi. Coins stay for the rest of the day.

Show

Projector

Mounted overhead. The image is turned 180° and masked to the pond's shape.

Feel

The pond

Water, koi and sound respond in real time, under your hand.

06What changed, and what it cost

I tried three art styles and moved the pond from a laptop to a table to the floor. Each move forced a change, and each change gave something up.

01

Painted water instead of sand particlesThe blue anime look read best projected on a floor. The particle version I built first was set aside.

02

A phone instead of a KinectThe Kinect v2 wouldn’t run reliably on macOS. An iPhone on the floor with MediaPipe did, but it has to be recalibrated whenever it moves.

03

Working within a free licenceTouchDesigner’s non-commercial licence caps textures at 1280px, so I repacked the koi to fit. Each fish has less detail up close.

07The film

Real footage from the day first, then concept renders of where the pond could go next. I edited it and made the music.

08 · What I learnedWhat stayed with me most was seeing something we made put a smile on people's faces, even for a few minutes.

It also showed me the kind of work I want to do: experiences that go beyond a screen, that people can walk into, touch and feel something through. Visuals, sound, technology and physical space together.
