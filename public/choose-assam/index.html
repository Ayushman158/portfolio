When the flood leaves the headlines, the work of reopening begins. Tata Tea gives its own media to the makers of Assam, and asks people to buy, not donate.

The problem

Assam's floods are covered as a tragedy, and forgotten as an economy.

  1. 01Attention is short. The news moves on in days. Clearing silt, repairing looms and winning back customers takes months.
  2. 02Charity sets the wrong role. Appeals cast Assam as a victim and the audience as a donor, and the relationship ends with the payment.
  3. 03Makers have no reach. Weavers, grocers and homestays can't reach buyers outside the state when they reopen.

A flood gets ten days of news. Reopening takes ninety.

Insights

People want to help. They just don't want it to end at a donation.

  1. 01A purchase keeps the relationship going. A donation is one moment. An order brings the buyer back, and the maker along with them.
  2. 02Tea is already the bridge. Assam reaches most Indian homes every morning, in a cup. The link exists; it just isn't visible.
  3. 03Dignity comes from trade. Makers want to be seen as producers with something to sell, not as people who need saving.

Producer,not victim.

Tata Tea hands over its packs, billboards, shelves and app slots to makers for ninety days.

Why Tata Tea

The brand already carries Assam into the home.

  1. 01Assam is in the product. Tata Tea Premium is a strong Assam CTC blend, and Tata's tea business grew out of Assam's estates.
  2. 02It has earned a public voice. Jaago Re showed the brand can speak on civic issues and still be believed.
  3. 03It owns the media. Packs, shelves, outdoor and digital reach at national scale. That's the infrastructure Choose Assam hands over to makers.
  4. 04“Desh ki Chai” fits. A national daily ritual is the right place for one region's recovery to become everyone's business.
