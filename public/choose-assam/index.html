After the flood, Tata Tea hands its media to the makers of Assam. Behind the campaign, a system tracks each maker’s recovery and decides how people can help: lend, pre-order or buy. Never donate.

Context

Every monsoon, floods in Assam shut workshops, shops and homestays. The news moves on in days. Reopening takes months.

Problem

Flood appeals ask for donations. That casts makers as victims, ends the relationship at the payment, and does nothing to bring their customers back.

What I designed

A campaign, a maker platform and the rules between them. Every piece of Tata Tea’s media carries one QR. It lands on verified makers, and each maker’s status decides what money can reach them.

Status

Speculative. An M.Des project for Designing Communication Ecologies at UPES. Not tested with makers or buyers; makers and figures are illustrative.

The problem

Assam's floods are covered as a tragedy, and forgotten as an economy.

  1. 01Attention is short. The news moves on in days. Clearing silt, repairing looms and winning back customers takes months.
  2. 02Charity sets the wrong role. Appeals cast Assam as a victim and the audience as a donor, and the relationship ends with the payment.
  3. 03Makers have no reach. Weavers, grocers and homestays can't reach buyers outside the state when they reopen.

A flood gets ten days of news. Reopening takes ninety.

Insights

People want to help. They just don't want it to end at a donation.

  1. 01A purchase keeps the relationship going. A donation is one moment. An order brings the buyer back, and the maker along with them.
  2. 02Tea is already the bridge. Assam reaches most Indian homes every morning, in a cup. The link exists; it just isn't visible.
  3. 03Dignity comes from trade. Makers want to be seen as producers with something to sell, not as people who need saving.

Producer,not victim.

Tata Tea hands over its packs, billboards, shelves and app slots to makers for ninety days.

Why Tata Tea

The brand already carries Assam into the home.

  1. 01Assam is in the product. Tata Tea Premium is a strong Assam CTC blend, and Tata's tea business grew out of Assam's estates.
  2. 02It has earned a public voice. Jaago Re showed the brand can speak on civic issues and still be believed.
  3. 03It owns the media. Packs, shelves, outdoor and digital reach at national scale. That's the infrastructure Choose Assam hands over to makers.
  4. 04“Desh ki Chai” fits. A national daily ritual is the right place for one region's recovery to become everyone's business.

The system

The media brings people in. The platform decides where their money can go.

Every piece of Tata Tea’s media carries the same QR. It lands on the maker platform, where each maker’s status sets which actions are open. The maker collective verifies makers and posts their updates, and those updates feed back into the pack and social.

The status engine

Money unlocks as a maker moves from Closed to Open.

This is what lets someone trust where their money goes. Nobody can buy from a maker who can’t ship yet, and loan repayments only start once the maker is selling again.

Backing makers

Keep the idea of investing. Change the structure.

Investing in a maker sounds right, but equity crowdfunding isn’t legal in India. SEBI calls it “unauthorised, unregulated and illegal”, and its 2014 proposal is still not regulation. So backing runs through three routes that are legal.

RouteWhoHow it worksLegal basis
Pre-orderAnyonePay now, receive the goods later. It funds the maker’s working capital.Reward crowdfunding, legal without sector-specific rules (InCorpX)
LendIndividualsSmall loans through a licensed P2P partner. Rang De is the model.RBI NBFC-P2P licence; escrow is mandatory and no repayment guarantee is allowed (Rang De, IES)
Back the collectiveTata and institutionsSubscribe to zero-coupon zero-principal (ZCZP) instruments of a partner NPO listed on the Social Stock Exchange.Since May 2026, up to 10% of a company’s CSR obligation can go to ZCZPs (S.S. Rana)

Backing the collective gives Tata a regulated, auditable way to anchor the fund, a stronger story than “we donated”.

What builds trust

  1. 01Verification. The collective checks every maker in person before listing. Each listing shows their Udyam registration and any GI tags.
  2. 02Milestone release. Money unlocks by status, as above. Nothing reaches a stage the maker hasn’t reached.
  3. 03A public ledger per maker. Orders fulfilled, money received and used, repayments made, and photo updates at 30, 60 and 90 days.
  4. 04Risk labels and a grievance path on every loan, because the platform can’t guarantee repayment.

Identity

One wordmark that never changes, and a craft band that changes with every edition.

The gamosa can’t carry the whole identity. Each pack edition takes its band from one community’s own material and links to that community’s makers on the platform. The pack becomes a series worth collecting, which gives people a reason to come back.

Constraints and tradeoffs

What I chose, and what it cost.

ConstraintWhat I choseWhat it cost
A brand can’t be seen profiting from a disaster, and charity casts makers as victims.Commerce: buy, pre-order or lend. Never a donate button.Harder to explain than “donate”, and it only works for makers who can sell again.
Makers recover at different speeds.A status for every maker that sets which actions are open.Early on, most makers aren’t open yet, so lending and pre-orders have to carry the first weeks.
Equity crowdfunding is illegal in India.Pre-orders, a licensed P2P lender, and ZCZPs on the Social Stock Exchange.Backers get no upside, and lending needs a licensed partner that can’t promise repayment.
Packs, shelves and billboards can’t be reprinted every week.One QR on every surface. What changes lives on the platform.It depends on the collective updating statuses every week, or the pack points at stale news.
A tea brand using tea-community imagery.A co-authored edition with artists credited and paid.Slower, and it needs community partners before the edition can exist.
